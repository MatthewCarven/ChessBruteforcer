The rest of a node: where a search node's time goes outside the network, h1024-3m, the engine at 4ecea62 (AVX-512,
64-byte alignment and the lazy update in), 2026-10-20 10:01-10:15. The laptop (i9-11900H, .NET 8.0.32), on AC, with
the bot OFF (Matthew stopped it just before 09:52, which freed memory; nothing else heavy running): quiet-laptop figures, and the
rounds agree to 1-2 ns. One thread at below-normal priority, timed by the thread's own CPU time (QueryThreadCycleTime
at the 2.496 GHz TSC). The engine itself was not changed: a scratchpad copy with hooks (search-split/split-patch.py).

Method. bench's six positions at depth 9, a fresh 16 MB table for each search (the bot's tables object over an empty
folder: it answers as the bot's does past five pieces or with castling rights). Per position: a recording run notes
every answer (evaluations, InCheck, PickNext's choice, table probes, repetition and tablebase answers, the move lists);
a check run confirms the network gives the recorded values; then 6 rounds (+1 dropped) of arms, rotated. "none" is
the search with no network work (evaluations replayed, no accumulator pushes): the rest of a node. A removal arm is
"none" with one piece's answers replayed instead of computed, on the identical tree (checked every run: the same
nodes, every replayed stream read exactly to its end). A doubling arm does one piece twice. Before timing, every arm
runs twice at depth 6, so the JIT compiles tier 1 from a profile of all of them (without that, a depth-5 trial was
off by up to 2x). Removal is the in-place cost; doubling reads lower (the second call finds warm caches and trained
branch predictors: 0.7x for generation, 0.88x for InCheck), a floor.
