## The rule, shared

`tune positions` and `nnue data` both walk games through one function now (Core/Records/QuietPositions.cs):
after move 8 (ply 16 on), more than 5 pieces, side to move not in check, neither the move before nor the move
played a capture or a promotion. The score is Lichess's after the move before, i.e. the position's own; a quiet
position with none is left out. Games: rated (Event), from the standard start, analysed (Evals tag), both
ratings given, a result. Against the tuner on real data (first 100,000 of September, above): `nnue data
--speeds blitz,rapid,classical` writes exactly the 1,999,783 positions `tune positions --min-elo 0 --per-game
1000 --lambda 0` does, plus 104,589 mates the tuner skips. An outside number agrees too: sf2's run (WORKLOG
2026-10-15) used 6,811,130 blitz-or-slower games; here 6,823,523, the 12,393 more being games whose only scored
quiet positions are mates (the first 100,000 had 126 such in 76,416, which predicts ~11,300).

## The record (32 bytes; NNUE.md "Training" has the table)

  occupancy u64 at 0 (a1 = bit 0); 32 pieces as 4-bit codes at 8, in square order, low half first; flags u8 at
  24 (1 black to move, 2 mate); result for white u8 at 25 (0, 1, 2); white's score i16 at 26; game number u32
  at 28. Piece codes: colour x 8 + type (white 1-6, black 9-14). Every field is on its own alignment, so the
  trainer can view a batch as int64 / int16 / int32 without copying; that is why the result sits before the
  score, not after it as NNUE.md first listed them. Castling rights, en passant and the clocks aren't stored.
